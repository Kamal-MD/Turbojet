**What was done:** Calculated Compressor Outlet Temperature, Material to use for Compressor to Chamber connection, Determined Compression Ratio and Energy the compressor requires\
**Details:**
+ The material to use for the compressor outlet to chamber inlet connection will be Aluminum, as it can withstand 600 degrees celsius and the compressor discharge temperature is unlikely to exceed 150 degrees (This decision is subject to future change)
+ To determine what material to use for the compressor outlet to chamber inlet connection, I had to determine Compressor Outlet Temperature. This was achieved by using the following equation:

  $$
  T_2 = T_1 \left[ 1 + \frac{PR^{\frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma}} - 1}{\eta_c} \right]
  $$

  Where:\
  T1 = inlet air temperature (in Kelvin(K), ~ 288K on a normal day)\
  T2 = compressor discharge temp (in Kelvin)\
  PR = Pressure Ratio (or Compression Ratio)\
  gamma (y) = specific heat ratio for air (approximately 1.4)\
  n_c = compressor isentropic efficiency (meaning how efficient it is and how much goes towards internal losses such as turbulence and friction), typically around 0.65 - 0.75 for a turbocharger like this

  But keep in mind that the compression ratio (PR) has not been decided at this point (we need to determine a ratio where this specific turbo can run efficiently)
  
+ So, I found a compressor map for HX35 (which was unofficial but detailed) in the following link: (https://www.dsmtuners.com/threads/how-to-read-the-hx35-hx40-compressor-map-s.362599/)\
  This is it:![HX35 Compressor Map](/images/HX35_compressor_map.png)
+ In reference to the map, we can see that an optimal efficiency of 76% is achieved at between a PR of 2.2 and 2.4. We will pick PR of 2.4\
  By inputting PR = 2.4 into the above equation, we get T2 ~ 400 Kelvin (specifically is 397K) which is around 127 degrees Celsius
+ Finally, we determined the work the compressor requires through the following equation:

$$
W_{\text{compressor}} = \dot{m}_{\text{air}} \cdot c_p \cdot (T_2 - T_1)
$$

Where: 

m(air) = mass flow rate of air (which I picked to be 28 lb/min or 0.2117 kg/s by looking at the map intersection of PR=2.4 at ~76% efficiency)\
c_p = specific heat for air ( around 1005 J/Kg*K)

And thus we get Wcompressor = 0.2117 kg/s * 1005 J/Kg*K * 112 K = 23.8 KW (KiloWatts)
