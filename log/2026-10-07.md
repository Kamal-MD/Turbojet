**What I did:** Calculated the stoichiometric Fuel-to-Air ratio for propane (FAR = 0.0642) and the overall equivalence ratio (0.19) that the combustion chamber should run on\
**Details:** To determine the amount of fuel and air to inject, as well as the percentage of air in the chamber zones (in the future), we need the stoich. FAR and overall equivalence ratio:
+ C₃H₈ + 5 (O₂ + 3.76 N₂) → 3 CO₂ + 4 H₂O + 18.8 N₂ (O2 is about 21 percent of air, meaning nitrogen gas is about 79 percent; therefore, there is about 3.76 N2 for 1 O2 (in mols))
+ Using the respective molar masses of the reactants, we see that m(C₃H₈) = 44.1g while m(air)= 686.5g. So stoichiometric FAR = 0.0642
+ This means that 1 kg of air burns around 64 grams of propane
+ The equivalence ratio φ is actual FAR/stoichiometric FAR (where if φ=1 combustion is stoichiometric, if φ<1 it is lean or has leftover O2, if φ>1 it has unburned leftover fuel)

Thus, we have to get FAR_actual:
+ FAR · LHV · η_b = (1 + FAR) · c_p · (T_out − T_in)  (where we have T_in = T2 = 400K or 126Celcius, and T_out = T4 = 880K or 606.7C)
+ LHV = Propane's Lower Heating Value = 46.35 (MJ/Kg); LHV is the amount of heat released when a fuel is completely burned
+ η_b(combustion efficiency) = 0.95 (This is an assumption, since we can know when we run experiments)
+ c_p (average specific heat of the hot gas): ~1.1 kJ/kg·K over this range
+ So, FAR_actual (overall) is around 0.012
+ φ_overall = FAR_actual(overall)/FAR_stoichiometric = 0.012/0.0642 ~ 0.19
  
**Problem:**
+ Propane's lean flammability limit (you could think of it as the equivalence ratio, or percentage of stoichiometric fuel needed to sustain combustion) is φ=0.5, or around 32 grams of propane
+ So we must do 2 contradictory things: Operate chamber at a φ that sustains propane's combustion and have it reach the turbine at φ_overall = 0.19 to avoid damaging it
+ Common practice in dealing with this (as is seen in commercial turbojets) is using zones to admit the air in stages, where the majority of combustion happens in the primary stage and the rest of the stages are used to finish combustion and cool it down
