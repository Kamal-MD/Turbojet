**What I did:** Determined what the chamber inlet diameter should be\
**Details:**
+ Determined air density at compressor discharged since mass flow alone doesn't tell you velocity through a duct, you need density too. Used the ideal gas law: ρ = P / (R × T)  
Where:\
P = compressor discharge pressure = PR × P1 = 2.4 × 101,325 Pa ≈ 243,180 Pa\
R = specific gas constant for air = 287 J/(kg·K)\
T = compressor discharge temp = 400K (calculated in the Sept 1 session)\
And thus we got: ρ2 = 243,180 / (287 × 400) ≈ 2.12 kg/m³
+ Unlike the chamber inlet (a free design choice), the compressor's own outlet diameter is fixed hardware — 52mm ID, per the HX35 V-band spec. So velocity here isn't chosen, it falls straight out of known geometry via continuity:\
ṁ = ρ × A × V  →  V = ṁ / (ρ × A)\
Where:\
m = 0.2117 kg/s\
A (52 mm compressor outlet inner diameter) = π × (0.026)² ≈ 0.002124 m²\
Which gives us: V2 = 0.2117 / (2.12 × 0.002124) ≈ 47 m/s
+ Then to determine velocity across the connector's changning diameter I used:\
ṁ = ρA₁V₁ = ρA₂V₂\
Density is approx. constant over this short, low-Mach run, so it cancels:\
A₁V₁ = A₂V₂  →  V₂ = V₁ × (A₁/A₂)\
Since A = π(d/2)², the π/4 factor is on both sides and cancels too — area ratio becomes a diameter-squared ratio:\
A₁/A₂ = (d₁/d₂)²  →  V₂ = V₁ × (d₁/d₂)²\
+ First I applied this math along the compressor outlet, which starts at V1=47m/s and 52mm diameter as d1 (directly at the compressor outlet with the 52mm inner diameter)
and then 75.5 mm as d2, which is the diameter of the silicone hose at the compressor end. Thus, we get the air velocity at the compressor end of the hose to be ~22.3m/s
+ Then to determine the velocity at the chamber inlet, we use V1 = 22.3m/s, d1 = 75.5 mm (which is the hose inner diameter on the compressor end), and d2 =50 mm, which is the inner diameter placeholder we put in September 1's session.\
As such we get the chamber inlet velocity at ~51 m/s
+ Since the the inlet velocity of 51m/s agrees with most DIY turbojet literature (I don't have exact sources for radially arranged turbocharger turbojets, only for axially arranged turbojets), we are keeping the chamber inlet diameter as 50 mm and will widen the silicone hose to 50.5mm inner diameter to fit over it
