## Logic behind the decisions

**What the pressure drop is.** Air in the annulus (between housing and liner) is at slightly higher
pressure than gas inside the liner. That difference is what pushes air through every hole.

**Why 4 %.** It's a trade-off. A larger ΔP gives faster jets that reach the center and mix well, but
loses more pressure, which comes out of turbine work and thrust. A smaller ΔP saves pressure but gives
slow jets and risks a hot core. 4 % is the middle of the usual 3–5 % range.
Schreckling comparison: *[to add]*.

**Hole area formula.** Each hole group passes air according to

$$\dot m = C_d \cdot A \cdot \sqrt{2 \rho \Delta P} \quad\Rightarrow\quad A = \frac{\dot m}{C_d \sqrt{2\rho\Delta P}}$$

**C_d (discharge coefficient).** The jet narrows just after it leaves the hole (vena contracta), so a
real hole passes less air than its geometric area suggests. 0.6 is the standard sharp-edged value.
Air flowing past the holes in the annulus lowers it; a thick plate relative to the hole raises it.

**Why zone areas follow the air split.** Every hole sees the same ΔP and the same air density, so every
hole passes the same air per cm² (≈ 0.0122 kg/s per cm²). Area is just air flow divided by that.

**Same area rule, different hole sizes.** Area is fixed by physics; count and diameter are a choice.
Jet reach grows with hole diameter, so each zone's holes are sized for its job:
- Dilution: few, large holes so jets reach the center and prevent hot streaks at the turbine.
- Primary: more, medium holes to feed the recirculation bubble evenly.
- Secondary: smaller holes.
- Cooling (later): smallest, so air stays along the wall instead of penetrating.

**Primary air assumption.** All primary air enters through liner holes (no swirler). Consistent with the
current design: air enters the annulus from a side inlet, propane enters at the center of the flange.
