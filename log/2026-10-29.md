## 1. Redesign: two-piece liner with an overlap film joint

Replaces the belled-joint / three-section design.

- **Head ring:** 63.5 × 2.5 mm tube, welded to the head plate.
- **Main liner:** 76.1 × 2.5 mm tube that slides over the ring.
  - The radial gap between them is the **film slot**: (71.1 − 63.5) / 2 = **3.8 mm**.
  - The cooling holes in the main tube feed the slot. The film exits at the end of the ring and runs along the liner's inner wall.
- **Joint 2 and the 88.9 mm section are dropped.** My wall-temperature estimates show the secondary and dilution walls survive without film. Joint 2's 5 % of the air goes back to dilution (58.5 → 63.5 %).
- **Fabrication:** everything is cut and drilled from standard 304 tube. No belling or forming.

**Area check:** primary 331.8 + secondary 222.7 + dilution 1099.6 + cooling 88.0 = **1742 mm²**, against a target of 1741 mm².

## 2. Decisions and the reasons behind them

### Head joint

**Bolt position.**
- **Problem:** the first layout put the M4 bolts at x = 5 mm, which left about 0.75 mm of metal to the tube end. That would tear out, and it also sat in the ring's weld bead.
- **Decision:** lengthen the overlap and put the bolts **upstream of the cooling row**, in the dead-end part of the gap, so the spacers stay out of the film. `ring_L` = 30, `x_bolt` = 9, `x_cool` = 18. The film now exits at x = 30.

**Gap at the head plate.**
- **Problem:** the 2 mm gap between the head plate and the main tube was an open annular slot of about 450 mm², five times the cooling-hole area. Most of the film air would have bypassed the cooling holes, taking air from primary and dilution, and the "dead-end" spacer zone would have had flow through it.
- **Decision:** butt the main tube against the head plate on a **thin graphite gasket ring**. `liner_start` = `tube_gasket_t`, and `overlap_L` = `ring_L − liner_start`.

**Match-drilling.** The M4 bolts are radial, so they locate the tube but don't clamp it onto the gasket. Push the tube firmly onto the gasket, then drill the 3 bolt holes through liner, ring and spacers in one setup. This guarantees the holes line up.

**Bolt hole and cut length.**
- `bolt_hole_d` = **4.5 mm** (clearance for M4).
- `main_L` = `liner_L − liner_start` is the cut length for the shop.

**Ring weld.** The weld must not sit in the film gap. Either grind the outside fillet flush, or weld from the inside. Both go on the drawing; the shop decides. Electrode: **E308L** everywhere. All flanges are 304.

**Spacers.** Cut them to the **measured** gap on the real tubes, not the CAD value. Tube OD tolerance and ovality can shift the gap by several tenths of a millimetre.

### Holes

**Hole sizing.**
- **Decision:** all hole diameters are set directly as round drill sizes. The `A_` values became check values showing the actual area and % error against target.
- **Final layout:**

| Group | Holes | x (mm) | Area error | Pitch ratio (on ID) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cooling | 28 × 2.0 | 18 | +1.0 % | 4.0 |
| Primary | 10 × 6.5 | 62 | +0.9 % | 3.4 |
| Secondary | 14 × 4.5 | 115 | +1.5 % | 3.5 |
| Dilution | 2 rows × 7 × 10.0 | 145 / 170 | −0.5 % | 3.2 |

**Cooling holes changed from 36 × 1.75 to 28 × 2.0.** Hand-drilling 1.75 mm holes in work-hardening 304 breaks drills. The area stays the same within +1 %.

**`x_primary` stays at 62 mm.** The 42 mm value only existed to clear joint 2, which is gone. 62 keeps the recirculation zone clear and sits downstream of the film exit at x = 30.

**`x_secondary` moved from 100 to 115 mm.** The head section now ends around x = 100. This keeps the row clear of the step plate.
- **Cost:** the burnout length to dilution drops from 45 to 30 mm. It's probably fine, since equilibrium CO is low; CFD or Cantera should confirm it.

**Hole clocking (rotation of each row):**
- **Problem 1:** Claude Code's first export put every row at 0°. All 14 dilution holes sat directly behind secondary holes.
- **Problem 2:** the first fix put dilution 1.29° off primary, which is effectively in line.
- **Final:** primary 0°, secondary **12.857°**, dilution row 1 **2.5714°**, dilution row 2 **28.2857°**.
  - This gives primary/secondary and primary/dilution separations of 2.571°, the best possible with 10 and 14 holes per ring. Secondary/dilution is 10.286°.
  - The cooling row is **exempt from the clocking rule**: it feeds the film slot and doesn't inject into the gas, so it leaves no wake.

### Housing, head end and flange

**Housing removable.** The housing is **not** welded to the head plate. A bolted flange joint with a gasket lets the liner come out with the head plate for inspection, and keeps the M4 nuts reachable.

**Flange.**
- **Decision:** a standard **EN 1092-1 PN6 DN125** flange pair: a blind flange as the head plate, and a slip-on flange on a **139.7 mm** (DN125) head pipe. This needs no machining.
- **Key dimensions:** 240 mm OD, 200 mm bolt circle, 8 × M16, 18 mm bolt holes.
- **Bolt clocking:** the bolts are clocked so the inlet stub sits halfway between two of them.
- **Thickness:** `flange_t` = **15 mm** as a placeholder until the bought flange is measured. It only sets the stub position.

**Stub position.**
- **Problem:** the flange bolt hardware collided with the inlet stub.
- **Decision:** make the stub position follow the flange.
  - `stub_x` = `fl_gasket_t + flange_t + hw_clear + stub_OD/2`, with `hw_clear` = 19 mm (washer + nut + 3 mm).
  - `head_L` = `stub_x + stub_OD/2 + 10 mm`.
- **Final:** `stub_x` = **61 mm**, `head_L` = **96 mm**.

**No cone and no bought reducer.**
- Use a **flat annular step plate**, 4 mm 304 sheet, welded to both pipes.
- **Keep the 108 mm housing.** The annulus air speed is what cools the uncooled secondary wall; a 114.3 or 139.7 mm housing would make it run hotter.
- `cone_L` is replaced by `step_t`.

**Inlet stub.**
- **Size:** 50 mm OD / 45 mm ID, so my existing **50.5 mm ID silicone hose** fits.
- **Cost:** inlet speed rises from about 51 to **62.9 m/s**.

### Slip sleeve (turbine end)

- **Radial clearance:** `slip_radial` = **0.5 mm** kept.
- **Axial clearance:** `slip_axial` = **5 mm** kept.
- **Liner growth:** the liner grows **3.14 mm** long when hot, leaving 1.86 mm of axial clearance.
- **Galling note:** apply anti-seize or graphite paste to the liner OD inside the sleeve.

## 5. Airflow notes for the CFD (head plenum)

The 139.7 mm head pipe is the plenum from the original design. It slows the stub jet before the air enters the annulus.

| Region | Air speed |
|---|---|
| Stub | ~63 m/s |
| Plenum | ~10 m/s |
| Annulus | ~26 m/s |

Things to check in the cold-flow CFD:
1. **Hole feed pressure.** The cooling and primary holes sit in the slow plenum, so they see higher static pressure than the secondary and dilution holes in the annulus. The difference is about 740 Pa, roughly 7.6 % of the liner ΔP. Expect about 4 % more air through primary and cooling than designed, with primary φ around 0.96.
2. **Step plate.** It's a sudden contraction. The flow separates at the housing wall for about 10–20 mm after the step, and the secondary row is only 15 mm past it.
3. **Stub jet.** The stub at x = 61 points almost straight at the primary row at x = 62. Primary holes on the stub side may get more air.

**Measure:** the mass flow through each hole row, and the variation around the circumference within each row.
**Fallbacks:** a small deflector in the head section (about 29 mm of radial room), or moving the secondary row further downstream.
